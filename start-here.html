Welcome: learn to read a chart

For learning only. This course explains how to read charts. It is not investment advice, a recommendation to buy or sell anything, or a promise of any result. Markets carry risk of loss. All charts are drawn examples, not real stocks.

The big idea

Every price on a chart is the result of buyers and sellers disagreeing in public. Rising prices mean buyers are more eager than sellers; falling prices mean the reverse. A chart is a record of that struggle, and price action is the skill of reading it directly: how the price moved, where it paused, where it was rejected, and how hard it pushed.

This course teaches that reading skill step by step. You do not need any background in markets. You need patience and a willingness to look at one chart carefully.

The promise, and its limit. By the end you will be able to describe what a chart is showing, in plain words, and explain your reasoning. We do not promise that reading a chart predicts what happens next. Nobody can promise that. Reading well helps you think clearly and manage risk; it does not remove risk.

What we will read

Four things, in this order, every time:

  1. One candle: its open, close, high and low (Class 1).
  2. The trend around it: is the market rising, falling or stuck, and where are the levels (Classes 2 and 3).
  3. Supporting tools: indicators that summarise past price (Class 4).
  4. The whole picture: a routine that puts the above together and keeps risk under control (Classes 5 to 8).

The habit we are building: structure first, patterns second, indicators last.

How each lesson works

  • Idea: one main idea in plain words.
  • Drawn examples: charts drawn for teaching, so you see the idea without distraction.
  • A table to keep for revision.
  • Quick quiz: three questions with the reason behind each answer.
  • An open question to answer in your own words before the next class.

Ground rules for studying

  1. Explain it back. If you cannot say it in your own words, you have not learned it yet.
  2. Context beats shape. The same candle can mean opposite things in different places.
  3. A pattern is a hint, not a promise. Wait for confirmation, and expect to be wrong sometimes.
  4. Indicators follow price. They are maths on the past, so treat them as support, not as the leader.
  5. Protect your capital first. Survive long enough to keep learning.
  6. Write things down. A journal turns experience into learning.

Words you will meet

What this course is not

  • Not a tip service. We never say what to buy or sell.
  • Not a promise of profit. Claims like "high probability" or "wins more than half the time" are one teacher's opinion, not facts, and we treat them that way.
  • Not a shortcut. Reading charts is a skill, and it grows with careful practice.
