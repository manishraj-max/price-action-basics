Reading candlesticks

For learning only. This lesson explains how to read charts. It is not investment advice, a recommendation to buy or sell anything, or a promise of any result. Markets carry risk of loss. The charts are drawn examples, not real stocks.

By the end you can:
  • read open, high, low and close from one candle
  • tell a bullish, bearish and neutral candle apart
  • explain why the same shape can mean opposite things
  • say why a single pattern needs the next candle to agree

1. Price action in one idea

A price moves from one point to another in some particular way: fast or slow, in a straight line or with pauses. Studying how it moved is called price action. The candle is the basic building block.

2. One candle, four prices

A candle summarises one time frame. A 5-minute candle shows what happened in those 5 minutes:

  • Open: the first price
  • Close: the last price
  • High and Low: the extremes, drawn as thin lines called wicks

The thick part is the body, between open and close.

CandleRulePlain meaning
Bullish (green)close above openbuyers won this period
Bearish (red)close below opensellers won this period
Doji (grey)open ≈ closeneither side won; indecision

3. The same shape can mean opposite things

Look at a candle with a small body and a long lower wick, at least twice the body. It shows that sellers pushed the price down during the period, then buyers pushed it back up. What that means depends on what came before.

Hammer: after a fall

Hanging man: after a rise

The idea to remember: the pattern is a clue, and the trend before it gives the clue its meaning.

4. Confirmation: wait for the next candle

A single-candle pattern is only a hint. Many traders wait for the next candle to agree before treating it as meaningful: a hammer followed by a green candle, a hanging man followed by a red one. If the next candle disagrees, the hint was weaker than it looked.

6. Two candles that stand out

  • Big bar: a candle much larger than the recent average. A lot of buying or selling happened.
  • Wick bar: a long wick with a small body. Price went somewhere and was pushed back.

Both say more when they appear at a meaningful place, for example near a level where price has turned before.

Quick quiz

1. A candle opens at 50 and closes at 58. What is it?

Close above open means buyers won the period: bullish.

2. A hammer and a hanging man look the same. What separates them?

The shape is identical; the trend before it gives it its meaning.

3. Why wait for the next candle after a single-candle pattern?

A single candle is only a hint; the next one tells you whether the market agreed.
